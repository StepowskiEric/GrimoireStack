# Six Thinking Hats — Reference Details

## Per-hat questions and rules

### White — Information and Data
- What data do we have? What do we need? What is missing? What is the evidence?
- No opinions, no interpretation, just facts and gaps; note what is not known as well as what is.

### Red — Intuition and Emotion
- What is my gut reaction? What does this feel like? What concerns or excites me without full justification?
- No data required, no justification required — these are signals, not conclusions; capture the reaction honestly even if it cannot be explained yet.

### Black — Caution and Risk
- Why might this fail? What are the risks and weaknesses? What assumptions could be wrong? What has gone wrong in similar situations?
- Not pessimism — disciplined caution; generate the most serious failure modes possible; do not balance with optimism here.

### Yellow — Value and Optimism
- What is the value? Why might this succeed? What are the strongest reasons to proceed? What opportunities does this create?
- Not blind optimism — disciplined benefit analysis; build the strongest case, not just the obvious benefits; do not introduce risk here.

### Green — Creativity and Alternatives
- What other approaches are possible? What if the current option were not available? What constraints could be relaxed? What combination would be better?
- Generate without filtering — the divergent phase; do not evaluate here; challenge assumptions not yet challenged.

### Blue — Process and Control
- What kind of thinking is needed? What is the goal? Which hats, in what order? What is the summary so far? What is the next step?
- Usually at the start (setup) and end (summary); can redirect unproductive thinking mid-session.

## Standard sequence

1. **Blue** — set the goal and plan the sequence
2. **White** — establish the information foundation
3. **Green** — generate alternatives before evaluating
4. **Yellow** — build the case for each option
5. **Black** — surface risks and weaknesses for each option
6. **Red** — capture intuitive signals after analysis
7. **Blue** — summarize and decide

Adjustments: Black before Yellow when risks are the primary concern; a second Green round after Black to solve the risks found; only three or four hats for simpler decisions.

```md
## Topic
<decision, proposal, or problem being analyzed>

## Blue Hat (Setup)
- Goal of this analysis:
- Hats to be used and in what order:

## White Hat (Information)

  - <fact>
- Data we need but do not have:
  - <gap>

## Red Hat (Intuition)
- Gut reaction:
- What concerns me without full justification:
- What excites me without full justification:

## Black Hat (Caution)
- What could go wrong:
  - <risk>
- Weak assumptions:
  - <assumption>
- Historical failures in similar situations:
  - <pattern>

## Yellow Hat (Value)
- Why this could succeed:
  - <reason>
- Value delivered if successful:
  - <value>

  - <case>

## Green Hat (Alternatives)
- Alternative approaches:
  - <alternative>
- What could be done differently:
  - <option>
- Constraints worth challenging:
  - <constraint>

## Blue Hat (Summary)
- Summary of thinking:
- Key tensions to resolve:
- Recommendation or next step:
```

## Failure modes this skill prevents

1. **One-mode collapse** — defaulting to one mode (usually caution or enthusiasm) and never genuinely engaging the others
2. **Argument-as-thinking** — framing the analysis as a debate between positions rather than a complete exploration
3. **Suppressed intuition** — skipping Red loses signals that pure data analysis misses
4. **Premature evaluation** — evaluating options before generating alternatives narrows the solution space

## Pairing guide

- **Inversion** — deepen Black with formal inversion to produce a failure map
- **Pre-Mortem** — feed Black output into a pre-mortem's failure-story generation
- **First Principles** — use under Green when alternatives seem exhausted
- **Bounded Self-Revision** — apply to the recommendation after the session

## Definition of done

Applied correctly when:
- each hat was worn fully and separately
- Black and Yellow were both applied (not skipped)
- Red captured genuine intuitive signals
- Green generated at least one alternative
- Blue summarized and identified the key tensions
- the final recommendation was informed by all relevant hat outputs
