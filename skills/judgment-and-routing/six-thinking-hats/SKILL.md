# Six Thinking Hats

**Most poor decisions come not from lack of information but from thinking modes mixed together.** Caution, optimism, data, and intuition expressed simultaneously produce conflicted, inconclusive output. Separate the modes — six hats, each worn fully and alone — and let each do its job without interruption.

## When to Use
- A decision risks collapsed thinking
- Reviewing a plan where both optimism and caution are relevant
- Generating alternatives rather than evaluating a single proposal
- Stuck in one mode at an impasse
- Balancing data-driven analysis with intuitive signals

Skip it: trivial low-stakes decisions, urgent incident response (speed beats perspective completeness), routine tasks with a clear correct answer.

## The Move

### 1. Blue — set up the session
State the goal of the analysis and which hats will be used, in what order. Blue is the process hat: it sets up at the start, summarizes at the end, and can redirect mid-session.

### 2. White — information only
Facts, data, and what is known — nothing else. What data do we have? What do we need? What is missing? No opinions, no interpretation, no caution; note what is not known as clearly as what is.

### 3. Green — alternatives before evaluation
Generate other approaches, relaxed constraints, and "what if the current option were not available?" before judging anything. Generate without filtering — this is the divergent phase; evaluation belongs to Yellow and Black.

### 4. Yellow, then Black — the case for, then the risks
- **Yellow** — build the strongest case: why this succeeds, what value it delivers, what opportunities it creates. Disciplined benefit analysis, not blind optimism.
- **Black** — then the risks: why this might fail, the weakest assumptions, what has gone wrong in similar situations. Disciplined caution, not pessimism. Each hat fully, never mixed — optimism does not balance Black; that is what the separate rounds are for.

### 5. Red, then Blue — intuition, then the decision
- **Red** — capture the gut reaction after analysis: what concerns or excites without full justification. These are signals, not conclusions — record them honestly, no data required.
- **Blue** — summarize: key tensions, what the hats revealed, the recommendation or next step.

## Rules
- **Do** commit fully to one hat before switching — a half-worn hat is a mixed mode.
- **Do** document each hat's output before moving on.
- **Do** treat Red as a legitimate signal, not noise.
- **Do** wear Black and Yellow both — skipping the uncomfortable hats collapses the analysis.
- **Do** use Blue to keep the session purposeful.
